Where is Barrow?

Barrow-in-Furness sits at the southern tip of the Furness peninsula in Cumbria, in the north west of England. It faces Morecambe Bay and the Irish Sea, with the hills and lakes of the Lake District not far to the north.

The town is made up of the mainland, Barrow Island in the middle, and Walney Island lying just offshore, separated by the Walney Channel. Because the peninsula is a dead end, Barrow feels a bit like its own little world, a long way from the big cities.

Getting here is part of the adventure. The main road in is the A590, and the railway runs along the Furness Line, which follows the coast and the edge of the bay with some lovely views.

Inside the submarine yard

Barrow is the home of British submarine building. Tap each class below to find out more.

Vanguard class

These large submarines carry the UK’s Trident missile system and were built in Barrow. They are being replaced by the Dreadnought class.

Astute class

A newer class of nuclear-powered attack submarines, also built in Barrow. They are packed with sonar and technology and are among the most advanced the Royal Navy has operated.

Dreadnought class

The next generation of ballistic missile submarines, currently being built at the yard. They will take over the Vanguard class’s role in the coming years.

Devonshire Dock Hall

A huge covered shipbuilding hall built in the 1980s. Submarines can be assembled indoors here, away from the weather, which is why the building stands out so much on the skyline.

Why Barrow?

The deep water, the long industrial tradition and a workforce with generations of skills have kept the yard here for well over a century.

Barrow AFC: the Bluebirds

Founded in 1901 and based at Holker Street, Barrow joined the Football League in 1921. They lost that status in 1972, then spent decades in non-league football before winning promotion back in 2020. Few comebacks feel as hard-earned as that one.

Barrow quiz

Frequently asked questions

Is Barrow in Cumbria or Lancashire?

Barrow is in Cumbria today. The Furness area used to be part of Lancashire, which is why some older maps and locals still mention it.

What is Furness?

Furness is the name of the peninsula Barrow sits on. It is the reason for the “in-Furness” part of the town’s name.

What is Barrow AFC’s nickname?

The Bluebirds, after the blue and white kits the team wears.

Can you visit Walney Island?

Yes. A bridge links it to Barrow, and it’s popular for walks, beaches and spotting birds.

What is the best time to visit?

Late spring to early autumn usually has the nicest weather for the coast and islands, but the area is worth seeing in any season if you wrap up warm.
