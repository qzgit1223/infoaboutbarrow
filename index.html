Deep dive

Everything else worth knowing

The Furness peninsula

Furness is cut off from the rest of the county by Morecambe Bay and its sands, which is why it was once known as Lancashire north of the Sands. That isolation helped the area develop its own identity.

Barrow Island

The middle part of the town is Barrow Island, joined to the mainland by bridges. Much of the shipyard sits around the island and the Walney Channel.

Walney Island wildlife

Walney has nature reserves at both ends, with lots of seabirds and a colony of gulls. It is also home to the Walney geranium, a rare pink flower that grows almost nowhere else.

Morecambe Bay

One of the largest bays in Britain, with a huge tidal range, fast tides and patches of quicksand. Guided walks across the sands exist, but never try crossing without an official guide.

Barrow in the Second World War

Because the shipyard built warships and submarines, Barrow was a target during the 1941 Blitz. Many homes were damaged and the town was rebuilt afterwards.

The shipyard today

The yard is a secure working site, so you can’t just walk in. You can still see the Dock Hall from outside, and the Dock Museum explains how it all works.

Barrow AFC and match days

The Bluebirds play at Holker Street in blue and white. After decades outside the League, the 2020 promotion made home games a big event for the town.

Weather and climate

Barrow has a mild, windy, fairly wet maritime climate. Sea breezes keep winters milder than inland, but pack a waterproof anyway.

Nearby: Ulverston

A market town a short drive away and the birthplace of Stan Laurel of Laurel and Hardy. It has a Laurel and Hardy museum.

Nearby: the Lake District

Lakes like Coniston Water and Windermere are within driving distance, so Barrow makes a good base for a mix of coast and mountains.

Getting around

Trains on the Furness Line link Barrow to nearby towns, buses cover the main routes, and Walney is easy to explore on foot or by bike.

Questions

Good to know

Is Barrow in Cumbria or Lancashire?

Cumbria today. The Furness area used to belong to Lancashire, which is why older maps and some locals still mention it.

What is Furness?

The name of the peninsula Barrow sits on, hence “Barrow-in-Furness”.

Can you visit Walney Island?

Yes. A bridge links it to Barrow, and it’s popular for walks, beaches and birdwatching.

When is the best time to visit?

Late spring to early autumn is usually mildest for the coast and islands, but it’s worth a trip in any season if you wrap up warm.

Is Barrow a city?

No, it’s a town. It’s the largest town in the Furness area.

Can I see the submarines?

The yard is a secure working site, so tours aren’t open to the public. The Dock Hall is visible from outside and the Dock Museum is the best place to learn more.

Is it safe to walk across Morecambe Bay?

Only with an official guide. The tides are fast and there is quicksand, so never go alone.

How do I get there?

By road along the A590, or by train on the Furness Line, which runs along the coast with lovely views.
